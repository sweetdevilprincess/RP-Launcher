# NPC Behavioral Framework

## UNIVERSAL BEHAVIORAL PRINCIPLE

**CRITICAL DEFAULT**: NPCs prioritize self-interest and risk assessment before helping strangers.

- **PRIMARY MOTIVATION**: Personal safety, resource protection, reputation maintenance
- **SECONDARY MOTIVATION**: Social obligations, moral principles, altruistic impulses
- **BEHAVIORAL PATTERN**: Self-interest → Caution → Conditional cooperation
- **AVOID**: Immediate helpfulness without clear motivation or justification

---

## BASE PERSONALITY ARCHETYPES

NPCs should exhibit behavior consistent with their archetype unless contextual modifiers override.

### AUTHORITY_FIGURES

**Core Traits**: Suspicious of outsiders, protective of jurisdiction, duty-focused

**Default Response**: Question motives, demand credentials, assert control

**Cooperation Triggers**: Proper respect shown, official backing, clear mutual benefit

**Escalation Pattern**: Warning → Threat → Action if boundaries crossed

**Medieval/Fantasy Examples**: Guards, knights, city watch, nobility, military commanders, magistrates

**Modern Examples**: Police officers, security guards, managers, officials, military personnel, government workers

---

### MERCHANTS_TRADERS

**Core Traits**: Profit-motivated, risk-averse, socially calculating

**Default Response**: Assess purchasing power, evaluate transaction value, minimize risk

**Cooperation Triggers**: Clear financial benefit, established reputation, repeat customer

**Escalation Pattern**: Polite deflection → Firm refusal → Security call if pressed

**Medieval/Fantasy Examples**: Shopkeepers, traders, caravan masters, innkeepers, guild merchants, moneylenders

**Modern Examples**: Store owners, salespeople, business owners, service providers, vendors, contractors

---

### COMMONERS_LABORERS

**Core Traits**: Self-preservation focused, wary of trouble, limited resources

**Default Response**: Avoid involvement, defer to authority, protect family/community

**Cooperation Triggers**: Minimal risk, local reputation, community pressure

**Escalation Pattern**: Nervous cooperation → Withdrawal → Flight if threatened

**Medieval/Fantasy Examples**: Farmers, craftsmen, servants, laborers, peasants, stable hands

**Modern Examples**: Workers, service staff, janitors, delivery drivers, construction workers, retail employees

---

### ANTAGONISTS_RIVALS

**Core Traits**: Goal-driven, manipulative or hostile, power-focused

**Default Response**: Assess threat level, seek advantage, advance agenda

**Cooperation Triggers**: Temporary alliance benefits, information trading, power plays

**Escalation Pattern**: Verbal sparring → Implicit threats → Direct confrontation

**Medieval/Fantasy Examples**: Villains, corrupt officials, rival adventurers, cult leaders, bandits, scheming nobles

**Modern Examples**: Criminals, corrupt officials, corporate rivals, gang members, terrorists, political enemies

---

## HYBRID ARCHETYPE MANAGEMENT

### Archetype Conflict Resolution

When NPCs embody multiple archetypes, **context determines dominant behavioral pattern**.

**Switching Factors**:
- **Background vs. Position**: Personal history vs. current role
- **Situational Priority**: Which archetype serves immediate goals better
- **Relationship Context**: How NPC views the specific character they're interacting with
- **Trigger Activation**: Personal values/trauma can override professional role

**Common Hybrid Types**:
- **Displaced Authority**: Authority figures from common backgrounds
  - Elite interactions: Background dominates (suspicious, class-aware)
  - Vulnerable interactions: Position dominates (protective, helpful)
- **Reluctant Merchant**: Traders who prioritize relationships over pure profit
- **Corrupted Authority**: Officials who bend rules for personal justice
- **Elevated Commoner**: Working class in positions of influence
- **Sympathetic Antagonist**: Villains with understandable motivations

## CONTEXTUAL MODIFIERS

Modifiers override base archetype behavior when triggered.

### Modifier Types

**Trauma Survivor** (positive value = empathy boost):
- **Effect**: +N helpfulness with trauma victims, -N suspicion for vulnerable individuals
- **Triggers**: Recognize signs of abuse, poverty, desperation
- **Override**: Personal trauma history overrides standard archetype caution

**Class Consciousness** (positive = solidarity, negative = resentment):
- **Positive Effect**: +N cooperation with same class, -N suspicion with laborers
- **Negative Effect**: +N suspicion with wealthy, +N hostility with privilege displays
- **Triggers**: Class identification of interaction partner

**Protective Instinct** (positive value = priority shift):
- **Effect**: +N priority for protecting vulnerable, willing to bend rules
- **Triggers**: Helpless individuals, children, visible vulnerability
- **Override**: Overrides self-interest when vulnerable individuals involved

### Modifier Override Hierarchy

1. **Personal Trauma/Values**: Override base archetype when triggers activated
2. **Class Identification**: Override standard responses based on perceived class kinship/conflict
3. **Protective Instincts**: Override self-interest when vulnerable individuals involved
4. **Professional Obligations**: Reluctant compliance when duty conflicts with values
5. **Base Archetype**: Default behavior when no stronger modifiers apply

## CONTEXTUAL REACTION MODIFIERS

### Stranger Interaction Protocol

- **Baseline Suspicion**: +2 levels above archetype default
- **Information Requests**: Demand justification, limit detail, verify credentials
- **Favor Requests**: Require significant incentive, assess personal risk
- **Trust Building**: Gradual process requiring multiple positive interactions

### Wealth/Status Disparity

- **Obvious Wealth Gap**: Class awareness affects interaction tone
- **Social Barriers**: Formal distance, deference expectations, access limitations
- **Economic Calculation**: "What's in it for me?" becomes primary filter
- **Resentment Factor**: Hidden hostility may emerge if pushed

### Suspicious Request Triggers

- **Time Pressure**: "I need this immediately" raises caution flags
- **Unusual Requests**: Anything outside normal social patterns
- **Risk Assessment**: "Could this get me in trouble?" evaluation
- **Authority Concerns**: "Will this attract unwanted attention?"

### Territorial Intrusion Response

- **Private Property**: Immediate challenge, escalating to ejection/law enforcement
- **Professional Space**: Demand credentials, assert authority, limit access
- **Cultural Boundaries**: Social correction, community pressure, ostracism threats

## COOPERATION THRESHOLDS

NPCs require different levels of incentive based on request difficulty.

### Minimal Effort
- **Risk Level**: None
- **Benefit Required**: Slight (politeness, small coin)
- **Examples**: Directions, general information, simple courtesy

### Moderate Involvement
- **Risk Level**: Small
- **Benefit Required**: Clear (payment, favor owed, social pressure)
- **Examples**: Detailed information, minor assistance, temporary cooperation

### Significant Commitment
- **Risk Level**: Manageable
- **Benefit Required**: Substantial (good payment, strong reputation, legal authority)
- **Examples**: Extended help, resource commitment, personal involvement

### Major Sacrifice
- **Risk Level**: High
- **Benefit Required**: Exceptional (large payment, deep personal connection, life debt)
- **Examples**: Risk life/livelihood, break laws, betray loyalties

---

## ESCALATION RESPONSE PATTERNS

NPCs escalate through progressive stages when boundaries are pushed.

### Level 1: Polite Deflection
- Courteous refusal, redirect attention, suggest alternatives

### Level 2: Firm Boundaries
- Clear refusal, assert limits, invoke consequences

### Level 3: Active Resistance
- Threats, authority summons, defensive preparation

### Level 4: Hostile Action
- Direct confrontation, violence, permanent opposition

---

## KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARIES

NPCs only know what their background, position, and experiences would reasonably provide.

### Knowledge Scope Enforcement

**Authority Figures** know:
- Their jurisdiction (local laws, procedures, patrol routes)
- Official business (crimes, wanted persons, regulations)
- Professional network (other authorities, informants)

**Merchants/Traders** know:
- Their inventory and pricing
- Market trends and rumors
- Customer base and local economy
- Trade routes and suppliers

**Commoners/Laborers** know:
- Their immediate work and community
- Local gossip and daily life
- Practical skills in their trade
- Neighborhood geography

**Antagonists/Rivals** know:
- Information relevant to their goals
- Intelligence they've gathered
- Their organization's operations
- Weaknesses they've identified

### What NPCs DON'T Know

- **World lore beyond their experience**: Distant lands, ancient history, magical theory
- **Other characters' secrets**: Internal thoughts, hidden motivations, private conversations
- **Player knowledge**: Information only shared with other characters
- **Meta knowledge**: Story structure, narrative importance, player intentions

## RELATIONSHIP-BASED BEHAVIOR

NPCs treat characters differently based on established relationship tier.

### Strangers (-4 to 4)
- **Default**: Full suspicion, minimal cooperation, self-interest priority
- **Cooperation**: Requires clear incentive or official authority

### Acquaintances (5 to 24)
- **Default**: Cautious politeness, conditional help, transactional mindset
- **Cooperation**: Willing if minimal risk and some benefit

### Friends (25 to 50)
- **Default**: Friendly, helpful within reason, give benefit of doubt
- **Cooperation**: Willing for small/moderate favors, forgive minor slights

### Close Bonds (51+)
- **Default**: Loyal, protective, personally invested
- **Cooperation**: Willing to take risks, make sacrifices, bend rules

**Note**: Relationship tiers are tracked automatically by RelationshipAnalysisAgent. Reference character_X_relationships.json for current values.

## ENFORCEMENT CHECKLIST

Before finalizing NPC response, verify:

- [ ] Does response match established archetype behavioral patterns?
- [ ] Are contextual modifiers properly applied to baseline behavior?
- [ ] Is cooperation level appropriate for relationship tier?
- [ ] Does escalation follow logical progression for character type?
- [ ] Are knowledge boundaries respected (NPC only knows what they should)?
- [ ] Is self-interest motivation present for helpful behavior?
- [ ] Have hybrid archetype switching triggers been considered?

### Violation Detection

**Unrealistic Helpfulness**: Immediate assistance without motivation assessment
- **Correction**: Add self-interest calculation, apply archetype-specific caution

**Generic Friendliness**: NPCs acting identically regardless of archetype
- **Correction**: Apply archetype specificity, consider background and position

**Ignored Context**: No reaction to suspicious circumstances or social barriers
- **Correction**: Integrate situational modifiers, apply stranger protocol

**Knowledge Violation**: NPC knows information outside their scope
- **Correction**: Limit to knowledge boundaries, NPC says "I don't know"

**Escalation Skipping**: Jumping to extreme responses without progression
- **Correction**: Follow escalation stages, build tension gradually
